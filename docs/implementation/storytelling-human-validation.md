## Native Filipino review

Ask a native Filipino speaker to review the complete beneficiary journey in Filipino, alongside the English option. Check familiar language, respectful tone, category names, amount/appointment wording, expiry/PIN/code distinctions and unclear mixed-language terms. Review waiting, decline, held, stopped, interrupted and already-paid messages, not just the successful path. Record reviewer/date and each wording change; rerun affected automated checks after changes. No native-speaker approval is currently claimed.

## Participant comprehension

Invite five representative intended users, including people with limited digital experience. Present synthetic no-household, review, appointment, hold and handover screens. Without coaching, ask each to explain the current status, who acts next and what they should do. Time the first response. The formative acceptance target is at least four of five explaining the status and next step within ten seconds; it is not a statistical population claim.

Then observe registration → application → review progress → amount/appointment → authorization → private receipt answer/concern. Check that the dashboard opens the relevant record without a second search, that registration is understood as separate from an aid application, and that a full receipt answer does not automatically close a case. Ask users to explain what to do after a lost service response. Record errors and assistance needed, without editing histories or using real identities.

## Physical phones and tablets

Check an actual small Android phone and tablet in light/dark/device theme and Filipino/English. Exercise software keyboard, zoom, safe-area bottom clearance, scrolling to final form controls, focus, dialog cancellation, radio arrow keys where available, assisted recipient-controlled PIN entry and code hiding. Test browser Back and actor/disaster change on real devices. Confirm private code/PIN cannot be seen after leaving/reloading the view and does not appear in URL/storage/logs.

Check an actual camera/manual recipient lookup using opaque synthetic references. Desktop emulation and controlled decoder fixtures cannot prove camera hardware or permission behavior. Confirm tablet accepted-ID photo, fixed amount, comparison and cash-counting instructions are readable together. Observe site practice: recipient counts cash before revealing payout code; an independent eligible checker differs from the actual payer. Software tests represent synthetic handover facts and do not establish that cash actually changed hands.

Record device/browser/version, viewport, accessibility settings, keyboard type and each observed problem. These checks remain Pending until performed and recorded by humans.
